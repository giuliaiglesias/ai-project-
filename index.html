MY AI PROJECT

AI in my everyday life

AI makes things easier. But how much help is too much?

For me, the most interesting thing about AI is also what worries me. It can help us do things faster, but I wonder what happens if we get so used to its support that we stop trying on our own.

01 / UNDERSTANDING AI

What is artificial intelligence?

AI is technology that uses information to make predictions, suggest things or carry out tasks. Many AI systems learn patterns from data. A spam filter is one example: it sorts emails by looking for signs that a message might be spam. [1]

Generative AI is the type that creates things like text, images or music when we ask it to. A chatbot generates answers using patterns learned during training. This can be useful, but it can also give us an answer that sounds right even when it is wrong. [2]

02 / BENEFITS AND RISKS

How could it help me?

I think one of the biggest benefits is saving time. AI can also give us ideas when we don’t know where to start. For example:

When studying

If I didn’t understand something from class, I could ask AI to explain it in simpler words. Then I would compare it with my notes to see if the explanation makes sense.

When working

AI could help turn messy notes into a first draft of an email. I would still read it before sending it, because it might miss something or use words I wouldn’t normally use.

When creating

It could suggest ideas for a project when I feel stuck. I think that is helpful, as long as I choose what fits and develop the idea myself.

My main concern

My main worry is getting too used to the help. If we ask AI to do every task, when do we practise doing it ourselves? I don’t want saving time to mean missing the chance to develop my own abilities.

There are other problems too. AI can make up information, repeat bias or create privacy risks. Fake voices and videos can be used to trick people. [2] So if a message sounded like someone I knew but asked for something unusual, I would contact that person separately to check.

03 / THE FUTURE

What could its future look like?

I think AI will become part of more things we do, including studying, working and communicating. It could help researchers and make information easier to access. At the same time, I worry that some jobs could disappear or change a lot. I don’t know exactly what that future will look like.

People will still need to supervise it. AI can also help create scam messages or support cyberattacks. [2] I think a tool that can take actions should need permission before doing something important, especially when a mistake could affect other people.

The electricity it needs is another issue. AI depends on computers in data centres, and those computers need power. The IEA’s 2025 report expects electricity use across all data centres to grow a lot by 2030, with AI a major reason for the increase. It also warns about pressure on electricity grids. [3]

That doesn’t mean blackouts will definitely happen. But we need to think about where the extra electricity will come from. I also think we should be able to continue working if an online service stops working. Having a backup matters when we depend on a tool every day.

04 / HOW I MADE IT

How this website was made

I began by writing a paragraph about what I think of AI. My main ideas were that it saves time, can help with creativity and could make us too dependent on its support. I asked ChatGPT to correct my English and help make the ideas clearer. It also added examples and source links.

The website starts with a short explanation of AI, then looks at studying, work and creativity. After that, it covers the risks and possible future. The layout is simple, with a light background, blue details and short paragraphs.

ChatGPT generated the HTML and CSS for the page. HTML puts the headings, text and links in order. CSS controls how they look and how the layout changes on a phone. I uploaded the files to GitHub and turned on GitHub Pages to publish the site.

AI helped check the factual information against OECD, NIST and IEA publications. I still need to read the sources myself and check the updated website on my computer and phone before submitting it.
